* * *

The greatest desire is to be free from desire. But the moment you see this, a deeper truth is revealed. Desire itself is trying to end desire. The mind creates the problem and then begins searching for a cure. It asks what is the cause of these drifts and what is the solution, but the very search is part of the same movement.

You say the mind lingers on what has already passed. That is its nature. It holds on, not because it is strong, but because it does not know how to let go. What you call afterglow is simply the echo of your own involvement. The more deeply you touched something, the longer its fragrance remains. There is nothing wrong in it.

Then you ask why you judge your own desires. Who is judging? If the desire arises within you and the judgment also arises within you, then you are dividing yourself unnecessarily. One part creates, another condemns. This division is the real conflict, not the desire itself.

And why this urge to fix, to overwrite? The same hands that build the sandcastle feel the need to destroy it. This is how the mind sustains itself, through constant movement. To leave something as it is feels like death to the mind. It must interfere, reshape, analyze. It cannot simply rest.

You ask why it cannot be left and forgotten. But forgetting is not something you can do. What is seen clearly disappears on its own. What is resisted, judged, or pushed away is what lingers.

Even the anger toward yourself is part of the same game. One part of you becomes the judge, another the accused. And you go on moving in circles, asking why the thought arises at all. Thought arises because it is its nature to arise. Just as the river flows, just as the wind moves, the mind thinks.

And you wish to be like a leaf floating in the river, untouched and unbothered by the path. But the leaf does not try to float. It does not wish to surrender. It simply moves because there is no one inside it trying to control the movement.

The moment you desire to be that leaf, you have already stepped out of the flow.

So nothing needs to be fixed. Nothing needs to be destroyed. Just watch. Watch the desire arise, watch the judgment arise, watch the urge to fix. In that watching, without choice and without interference, something begins to settle.

Not because you did anything, but because you finally stopped doing.
