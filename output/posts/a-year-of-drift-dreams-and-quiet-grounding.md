This year felt like a rollercoaster, much like the one before it. Apart from the memory of Ishaan being hospitalized, which remains etched deeply, the remaining months unfolded like a series of thrillers — unpredictable, intense, and emotionally charged. If there is one word that defines this year, it would be _relentless_. Not in action, but in dreams.

Dreams poured out of every little crab hole in my sleep. I would wake up with a heavy, curious heart, often convincing myself that it was just a sick, crazy dream. Searching for meaning online only made things worse — the answers never felt healthy. Yet, what I carry inside my mind, the stories and symbols I’ve hoarded silently, feel nothing short of a masterpiece. Chaotic, yes — but deeply mine.

This year, I consciously tried to avoid conflict. Not because I was weak, but because I was tired of chaos. Osho’s words played a quiet but powerful role in this shift. I wanted a forced change in perspective, and in some way, I punished myself by reading a book — something I had long avoided. It did exactly what it was supposed to do. It slowed me down. It made me observe. Some days, I felt hard as a rock; other days, I became a leaf floating atop a stream, surrendering myself to the direction of the flow.

I was lucky to spend a few days with my closest friends. It wasn’t long, but it was necessary. I’ve realized that goodbyes affect me deeply — perhaps more than I let on. That makes two emotional departures this year. As my friends often joke, moving on from something is probably the last thing on my bucket list.

Being a father brought me more joy this year than ever before. I loved teaching Ishaan his class syllabus, spending evenings watching him play, laugh, and giggle. I don’t want to be the kind of father who sends his child down with a caretaker and later compensates with affection through a WhatsApp status. What’s the point of realizing the value of time only after it has already flown past? I want to be present — now, while he still needs me. These days, Ishaan is, quite honestly, my pookie.

Stepping outside my comfort zone didn’t involve grand adventures. It was quieter than that. I read a book. I shopped a little for myself and for friends. I didn’t feel the urge to travel this year — the itch simply wasn’t there. Instead, I found profound joy in conversations with myself. I wrote more in my thoughts than on paper. Somewhere along the way, I also found new music — a small but meaningful victory after the drought of the previous year.

Looking back, this year didn’t give me answers. It gave me acceptance. It taught me that drifting isn’t always aimless — sometimes it’s necessary. Sometimes, healing doesn’t arrive immediately. It comes slowly, gently, when you stop fighting the flow and allow yourself to feel everything as it comes.

And maybe, that’s enough for now.
