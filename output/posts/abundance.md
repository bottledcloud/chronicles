I remember several moments in my lifetime, and few are stamped in my thoughts than my heart makes its easy to access because my mind would constantly think about it. Yesterday I was upset for a while thinking that I can no longer enjoy the smell of new curriculum books stacked on top of my table, skimming through chapters which I am going to learn once the vacation ends and that’s a massive loss once you level up in age. From getting kissed on your lips for the first time to saying one last goodbye as cab departs into distance, life demands not to carry all thoughts into the present and yet if I look back, I see and feel everything. When first love departed, I presumed that my life was at crossroads until I found love at another distance and once that departed I understood that I was never at crossroads, I need a solution and it was to not trip in love again.

First love, like everyone says, is cringe and cheesy because I was 19 back then. Being in love for a person like me attracted a lot of questions. One, being ugly and slim, is one thing, and secondly, dating a North Indian during college days was another thing. Love was all about hiding at every nook and cranny in the campus and spending time together, as there were not many smartphones back then. Plus, the campus was like a wide-open area with no entry or exit. Love was just like in the books – spending time under the trees, holding hands when no one was watching, and dancing together for any events. I was even summoned by the college principal just to lie to his face that there was nothing happening between us. But did that stop us from being in love? Absolutely not, because by the 3rd year, everyone in the batch had their respective pairs, and everyone was joyous over the college tours. Love was immense. I never left home without a kiss, and the first time I got a kiss on my lips was right in my house while my mom went to get a glass of water for her. Like I said, love was in abundance, and my first love was a roller coaster ride – from group studies to traveling together from Delhi to Kerala via Rajdhani Express for 3 days, to a surprise visit in Chennai as part of company training, to completing each other's diaries just to make sure love was in perfect balance. But fate had its own plan, as at one point, reality had to take a stand in between love and career. As time passed, things moved apart, and love became friends, and then friends became strangers, and then it became Instagram friends.

I chose to be alone for a while, even though it messed up my mind. I was distant from any form of love, and life became a routine. I tried to find some glimmer of happiness until a message came from miles away, from a country I usually only see during the football World Cup. Soon, that long-distance friendship became a routine, and slowly but steadily, I fell back into my pattern of falling in love again. With her, things were different. She was too young to be loved in the conventional way, so I had to imagine love being 5 years younger. This made me understand love in a completely different way. I realized I had a short temper, but I was willing to let go of things even if it hurt someone else. She taught me empathy and encouraged me to fill that void in me with love. Being in love with a Saurashtrian for more than 3 years, only to spend less than 48 hours together, was an unreal feat for me. The most cherished memory will always be holding her hand under the table while diverting her mom's attention to eat Kesari bath from Rajadhani Hotel in Chennai. This phase of love was the most emotional one, as emotions could only reach virtually. It can be considered the best one because I owe her everything for who I am today. She deserves credit for every Instagram caption I write, as I wrote the most love letters to her. A few words I still remember are "_Mogo thogo jukku opairas_".

Post this ordeal, I decided to ease down on social media and take a nosedive into focusing on my career goals. I started taking classes in Bangalore at a local institute to get an edge on the competition. But instead of career growth, I found love again. However, this time it wasn't me who initiated the conversation; it was a quote I had written on my book that caught that cute Tamil girl's attention. It started with her borrowing my books for missing the classes, and eventually led to her writing her number on my book. At first, I thought this was happening again and that I should have said no. But life seemed determined to throw me into love once more. With her, I roamed in and around Bangalore. Our love story was like something out of modern movies. We went on dinner dates, movie dates, and I hardly spent a weekend in my room talking to friends. Instead, I found myself at various cafés, swiping credit cards, and questioning if I could sustain this relationship without going broke. It was either the lecture or the love, and the time had come when I was about to do the Ladakh Expedition. When I returned from the trip, my thoughts had already shifted, leading to the impending breakup at Hole In The Wall Cafe, where we sat eating hot dogs and potato wedges. I am yet to forgive myself for this ordeal because I could have stopped at the moment where it all began. However, I was too curious to know how far it could go. From meeting her sisters and friends who kept asking me if I would marry her after dating for only a month, to discovering that her dad is a police officer in Bangalore, things became hard for me to contemplate. But by then, time had already decided a path for me—to walk away from club hopping and indulging in unhealthy habits every weekend. Love was there, but it was a little unhealthy.

Post Ladakh Expedition, my mind was fixated on traveling, and I spent more time writing on Instagram so that my mind could find peace. The moment you pen down your thoughts on paper, half of the headache vanishes. Loneliness overtook all other emotions, and love rusted at the windowpane for months until someone caught the attention of the stories I had been writing. This time we never named it love, but we knew we had a fire that couldn't be extinguished by words. This relationship also came from the North to the South. From watching Deadpool together to eating Ghee Rice and Boneless Chilli Chicken from Mughal, it was the start of something beautiful. She was my companion for everything, alongside my pet Oreo. We had a relationship that couldn't be named. Maybe I am the only one for whom she let her guard down and explained what was happening around her and in her mind. There were days I missed her, but as I said, time had different plans for this Mallu bird who flew away from my nest. But I always think and remember the days we spent together. She was a teacher for me from some perspectives. But, as I said, time and love are different for each other. No matter where you are, you will always feel that this feeling is temporary.

After that, I shifted to a new room and made a new set of friends. This change prevented me from overthinking because I found friends who needed someone new like me in their lives. Their lives had become routine, so I spent most of my time in a room inhaling cigarette smoke and endlessly drinking beer. However, this eventually ignited the desire to start writing again. I had stopped writing because of the impact it had on me and others, but life is circular and it always comes back to the starting point. Then, I encountered a person who was much older than me, someone who could challenge me in my thoughts and words. She was nine years older and her thoughts and perspective were impeccable because books were her life. She loved books and poetry, and her words reflected this passion. What she could convey in simple words was often difficult for me to grasp. This marked the beginning of something beautiful, but this time, I was like a student. We read books together, such as "Tuesdays with Morrie" and enjoyed moments of indulgence with coffee infused with Baileys Irish Cream Liqueur. Amidst the impending chaos, the days were good. She was the only person who ever gifted me a framed Porcupine Tree band poster, and it made me wonder if she truly knew me well. She was also the only person who gave me an original Rudraksha from Manali, even before I had been there. However, life had to pause for a while, and this avid Mallu reader had to take a leave. Our divergent choices gradually built a wall between us, and as time passed, we ended up on different paths. I have no idea what she is up to now, but I am quite certain she would be in a library, engrossed in reading more books. With her, my writing skills improved drastically, and for that, I am forever grateful. Whenever our paths cross again, I will always owe her.

Even though I long for a break from these trials and tribulations, having a companion, be it for a short journey or a long one, has its merits. Speaking of short journeys, I once met someone who was both wild and intelligent, someone who excelled in academics and had a passion for pilots. We spent a considerable amount of time together, from sneaking out of work to catch a movie at Central Mall to indulging in lunches at cafes in Sarjapur. Through this experience, I gained insight into the loneliness she felt as an only child and I tried my best to provide her with companionship. However, as they say, the path one chooses in life can inadvertently alter the course of others as well. While I wanted to stay in touch somehow, she flew away and I must shoulder the blame for it. At that time, I was caught up in a different company and failed to dedicate enough time. I should have offered a proper closure. Perhaps, one day, I will have the opportunity to explain these circumstances.

Talking about the upcoming person is very tough because it's very personal for me, and I did lose a lot of consciousness over her. If I am still not over someone in logical terms, then this is it because this was the first time, even without a thought, I fell in love. This was the time when I was the only one being in love, not from the other side. She was like a mirror image of what I am, and this was the first time I met someone who is crazy like me and who kissed me just because she missed me. At this point, I lost myself, and I had no control over what I am doing when I am next to her. I spend most evenings with her walking around the park without saying or uttering a single word. I would wait until she hugs me tight when it's time for me to head back home and would say I am already missed even before reaching home. The sad part of this relationship was that we figured out that we loved each other at the wrong time, and the timeline was already messy, and there was no going back for me. Only if I had the courage to ask like I always do, but this time I loved from afar, which made a lot of difference. Like I said again, due to an unlikely event in our life, we split ways without giving closure. From drinking tea under that red fruit tree to smoking menthol cigarettes under the rain, we could have been smiling still, but someday we will sit across tables and talk until we find a common point, only to realize we could have been friends. I do miss the fossette on your cheek, and you're the only person who made me smoke a menthol cigarette just because you like it.

Now let's take a step back. All of these are compilations of the memories that are still intact, and I do revisit them in my thoughts because these memories have shaped me and helped me understand my vulnerabilities. I never wanted to leave anything without closure, but not everyone thinks the same, and some don't like closures because life is an open-ended string. You will always find something to tie it to, whether it's a person or a rock underwater. That's life. No matter where you are now, you will always feel that this isn't where you are supposed to be. Everyone is caged by their own thoughts; the foolish ones believe life is free and boundless.
