Sometimes, sadness is when you realize that your favorite song has moved on in time—no longer your favorite—even without whispering a word. It reminds me that even non-living things can let you go. To what should I surrender now? Love is stuck like a nail under the foot—an irreversible attachment. What else is left to fathom? The winds of the monsoon don’t make me jealous anymore; they just drench the coat, not the heart. That remains bound—to fragments of speech thrown at me carelessly, like shrapnel from someone else’s chaos. Should I care to explain the lonely dreams? Where are they now? Have they too vanished, without saying goodbye? How poetic it all sounds when pain is voiced through rage and silence—like rays of light escaping through a narrow passage in a dark room. You all have left, leaving behind a pile of flesh that asks for nothing but a sliver of solace in this dark place I once called home.

I’ve stopped looking back, stopped trying to figure out what went wrong—because no adjustment now can alter the wreckage of the path already walked. The time around me is meant for eternity, unyielding and cold. So why turn around, why dig through old faces and fading thoughts that were always just jetsam in this endless voyage? Surrender to the love that walks beside you—yes—but know it limps, bleeding too. Find a shelter that can save you, even temporarily, and rest… just for a while. The journey is long and brutal. Like rubble once part of a mighty rock, now split and scattered—where even the roots of trees break through—you too must endure. Time does not beg you to stop. It is cruel in its forward march. And perhaps it’s unfair to halt, even briefly, to think—when you’ve always let your mind run into its own rabbit holes. So let it run, before your heart begins to feel again. Let no dawn or dusk judge you for your sins. Let no earth cause you to stumble when you dip your toes into its skin. You have been the sun. You have been the stars. These graveyards—no, they don’t haunt us for our sins. They are for the fools who were too afraid to try.
