In the dim streets of my unconsciousness, there lingers a version of me, drifting alone, crafting a canvas of familiar faces I once held dear. A mellow lo-fi track hums softly in the background, looping endlessly as I paint. With each finished portrait, the image drifts upward, taking the place of the leaves on a tree above. Now, the tree is adorned not with leaves, but with snapshots of those who once filled my life. I had imagined the tree would be heavy with a thousand memories by now, but instead, it holds just under a hundred—a number that feels small and unjust compared to the vast expanse of my recollections.

Perhaps it's time to replace these faces with places and objects—things that have anchored me through the years. Maybe by next summer, the tree will be ready, adorned with memories more tangible. The old plate my mom used to feed me on, my dad’s well-worn bike, or even my first AXN red cycle. But when I consider it, something feels off. Memories aren't just still frames; they're living, breathing fragments of time, always in motion. So, the tree shouldn’t just hold faces or objects, but pictures that capture the essence of those moments in transit—faces, places, and objects intertwined.

I can already envision how magnificent the tree will look by summer. Each picture will be a fragment of time, together forming a mosaic of life. The thought fills me with a quiet joy, as if I’m watching the past come alive in the leaves of that tree, gently swaying in the warm breeze.
